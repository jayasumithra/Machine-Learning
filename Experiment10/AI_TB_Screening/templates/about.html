Project Abstract & Executive Overview

Tuberculosis (TB), caused by the bacterium Mycobacterium tuberculosis, remains one of the world's deadliest infectious diseases. Chest radiography (X-ray) is the standard high-throughput screening modality endorsed by the World Health Organization (WHO) for early triaging. However, the global shortage of expert radiologists in high-burden regions leads to substantial diagnostic delays. This academic project introduces an end-to-end Computer-Aided Detection (CAD) prototype: “AI-Based Tuberculosis (TB) Screening from Chest X-Ray Using Deep Learning”. The system demonstrates the entire pipeline from raw image ingestion, bilateral lung preprocessing, deep feature extraction, transfer learning with EfficientNetB0, empirical test set evaluation, and Explainable AI (Grad-CAM) integrated into a responsive Flask web application.

End-to-End System Architecture
[Raw Chest X-Ray Radiograph] (PA/AP View)
                                                                ↓
[Image Preprocessor]          → Validate Integrity → Convert to RGB → Resize (224×224) → CLAHE Contrast → Normalize [0.0, 1.0]
                                                                ↓
[Deep Feature Extraction]    → EfficientNetB0 Backbone + GlobalAveragePooling2D → 1280-Dimensional Latent Embedding
                                                                ↓
[Transfer Learning Model]     → Batch Normalization → Dropout(0.3) → Dense(128, ReLU) → Dense(1, Sigmoid)
                                                                ↓
[Screening Prediction]        → TB Probability (%) vs Normal Probability (%) + Classification Threshold (0.5)
                                                                ↓
[Explainable AI (Grad-CAM)]  → GradientTape Gradients on `top_conv` → Spatial Weighting → Jet Heatmap Overlay
                                                                ↓
[Persistence & Audit]         → SQLite Logging (Date, Time, Probabilities, Execution Latency, Overlay Reference)

College Viva Voce Questions & Answers
Q1: Why use Transfer Learning with EfficientNetB0 rather than training a CNN from scratch?
Answer: Medical imaging datasets typically have limited sample sizes compared to massive consumer datasets. Training a deep network from scratch often leads to severe overfitting. EfficientNetB0 is pre-trained on ImageNet (1.4 million images), enabling it to reuse rich low- and mid-level feature representations (edges, textures, contours) via fine-tuning, achieving higher generalization while using far fewer parameters (~4M vs ~25M in ResNet50).
Q2: What is data leakage and how is it prevented in this pipeline?
Answer: Data leakage occurs when information from the validation or test sets contaminates the model training process, artificially inflating reported accuracy. In our system, the held-out test set is split before any data augmentation or parameter optimization. Data augmentations (rotations, zooms) are applied exclusively to training batches.
Q3: Why is Recall (Sensitivity) often considered more critical than Accuracy in TB screening?
Answer: In infectious disease screening, a False Negative (failing to identify an active TB case) can lead to disease transmission, permanent lung damage, or patient mortality. In contrast, a False Positive results only in a confirmatory secondary test (e.g. GeneXpert or sputum culture). Therefore, high Recall is prioritized to minimize missed infections.
Q4: What are the mathematical mechanics behind Grad-CAM?
Answer: Grad-CAM computes the partial derivative \(\frac{\partial y^c}{\partial A^k}\) of the target class score \(y^c\) with respect to each feature map \(A^k\) in the final convolutional layer. These gradients are globally pooled to form importance weights \(\alpha_k^c\). The weighted feature maps are summed and passed through a ReLU function to discard negative activations, highlighting only the spatial regions that positively influenced the prediction.
Limitations & Future Enhancements
System Limitations
  • Binary classification does not differentiate other pulmonary diseases (pneumonia, COVID-19, pleural effusion).
  • Screening model requires standard frontal chest radiographs; lateral views or pediatric radiographs may yield degraded results.
  • Operates purely on 2D radiographic projections without integrating patient clinical symptoms or microbiological data.
Future Enhancements
  • Multi-class pulmonary pathology classification across 14 thoracic disease categories.
  • Integration with DICOM medical imaging servers (PACS) and HL7/FHIR hospital protocols.
  • Vision Transformer (ViT) and Swin Transformer backbone comparisons.
  • Federated Learning for privacy-preserving collaborative model training across multi-center hospital networks.
